            Field notes

                About Tidepool

                Tidepool is an artificial-life simulation. Every creature carries a genome (body size, diet, muscle, eyesight, breeding strategy, even its own mutation rate) and a small neural network that turns what it senses into how it swims, turns and bites. Beyond a few newborn reflexes in the very first generation, none of that behaviour is scripted.

                Creatures that eat enough reproduce, passing on slightly mutated genes and wiring. When a lineage drifts far enough from its founder, it is recorded as a new species and named for its most distinctive traits: vorax for predators, velox for fast swimmers, minuta for the tiny.

                Reading the pool

                • Grazer. Colour marks its species; related species share similar hues. The tail beats harder as it swims faster.
                • Hunter. Meat-eaters grow mandibles, which snap open as they bite.
                • Faded body. Running low on energy.
                • Red ring. Just bitten.
                • Plankton and carrion. Plankton (green) blooms around drifting nutrient upwellings, the soft glows. Carrion (red) is what's left when something dies, and it slowly rots.

                Scale and time

                The slide is 1.6 mm across and creatures are 6–24 µm long. A day is 600 ticks, and plankton follows a 12-day cycle of bloom and lean season. Your pool saves itself in this browser and resumes when you come back.
